Manual · Viscera 2.9.0 · 4-Track

Guide

Viscera 4-Track turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into an overdubbing tape machine. Four tracks of tape in its memory, a 424-style preamp on the input and every channel, VU meters, and RESON to print a bounce through. This page covers installing it safely, getting sound in, how the sound flows, and what every page and control does.

01

Installing

Coming from Ravel? Go back to stock first

Don't install Viscera straight over Ravel, SLOOP or any other Felucca-based firmware. Handing over to M-VAVE's updater from those can switch the FM-1 off part-way through. Viscera's install page refuses to install from them for this reason.

  1. Open Ravel's install page and use Back to the stock firmware with M-VAVE's V15 file. That puts M-VAVE's own firmware back.
  2. Check that the FM-1 starts into M-VAVE's firmware.
  3. Then come back to Viscera's install page and install from there.
Early firmware

Viscera is being tested on hardware now. Back up first: save your projects and samples with M-VAVE's workbench, because switching firmware doesn't convert them. The 4-Track keeps its tape in memory: it is gone when the FM-1 switches off. Projects keep the desk (every setting, the robots), not the tape.

Steps

  1. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer. Switch the FM-1 on and connect it by USB. Close other MIDI software.
  2. On the install page, pick a version from the menu. The newest is first, and each has a line on what it changed. Press Use this version.
  3. Press Connect and allow MIDI access. The page says what the FM-1 runs now, and names the Viscera version if it's one.
  4. Press Install Viscera. Keep the cable in, the FM-1 on and the tab in front until it restarts, which takes one to three minutes.

Going back: the version menu still has the earlier Viscera (1.x, the live-capture PUTTY firmware). Put M-VAVE's V15 back installs M-VAVE's original firmware. M-VAVE's workbench can too. Projects saved with 1.x don't load in 2.0, and the other way round.

02

Quick start

  1. Send sound in. Pair a phone over Bluetooth and play something, or play into the FM-1 from a computer over USB (see Getting audio in).
  2. Track 1 is armed at start-up: its key (the lowest white key, F3) is lit.
  3. Press REC. The first take starts: the reels turn and the counter runs. Press REC again (or PLAY) to end it. That length is the loop, and it plays round.
  4. Arm track 2 (the second white key, G3) and disarm track 1 (F3). Press REC to punch in, and REC again to punch out. You can do this as often as you like, anywhere in the loop.
  5. Press EDIT for the MIXER: KNOB 1–4 are the four tracks' levels. Push a track's TRIM (EDIT again) until its lamp glows: that's the preamp warming it.
  6. Bounce (SEQ): the white keys F3–B3 pick the tracks to bounce, and REC prints one loop of them, through RESON, onto a free track.

03

Getting audio in

The FM-1 has no analogue input. Sound gets in two ways, and Viscera can use both at once. Everything about it is on one page: INPUT (EDIT, after the mixer's three views).

InputHow
BTA phone, tablet or computer plays music to the FM-1 as a Bluetooth speaker. Pair from the phone's Bluetooth settings and press play. Choosing BT (or BOTH) on the INPUT page switches Bluetooth audio on if it was off.
USBConnected to a computer, the FM-1 shows up as a USB audio device. Choose it as the output in your computer's sound settings or your DAW.
BOTHThe two together (the default).

Each source's button says what it's doing (BT: OFF, NO LINK, LINKED or PLAYING; USB: HOST or NO HOST), and its green lamp lights while audio arrives. The big VU meter shows the level going to tape. Aim for the needle around 0, with the OL lamp only flickering.

04

Signal path

BT / USB ─▶ TRIM ─▶ PREAMP ─▶ (RESON) ─┬─▶ armed tracks: DUB or REPLACE (DRIVE) └─▶ monitor ─────────────────────────────┐ │ TAPE 1–4 ─▶ head ─▶ (RESON, one track) ─▶ TRIM ─▶ PREAMP ─▶ LEVEL / PAN ─▶ ─┴─▶ PITCH ─▶ out SPEED: varispeed (the tape's mix) BOUNCE: the picked tracks ─▶ (RESON) ─▶ DRIVE ─▶ LEVEL ─▶ a free track, one loop
  • The tape: four mono tracks, 4-bit ADPCM in RAM, at the TAPE SPEED you choose. SPEED plays it faster or slower, pitch and time together, as tape does.
  • The preamps: the input's and each channel's. Clean below 0 VU; past it they round off softly, with a little second harmonic, as a pushed TASCAM 424 does.
  • RESON: an eight-band resonant filterbank, on one track's playback, on the input (it goes to tape), or on a bounce.
  • PITCH shifts the tape's mix without changing its time. The monitored input isn't shifted.
  • Robots move SPEED, PITCH, levels, pans and RESON on their own.

06

The tape (HOME)

The cassette window: the reels wind from left to right as the loop plays, the counter shows tenths of a second, and the loop's length sits under it. Below are the transport lamps, the TAPE SPEED switch, and the four tracks: each with its ARM lamp, its shape over the loop, where the tape is, and its level.

Knob
SPEEDVarispeed: half to double speed. Pitch and time move together, as tape. Recording slow and playing back at 1.00× is the old trick for chipmunks and octave-up parts.
PITCH±12 semitones on the tape's mix, without changing its time. At 0 it is out of the way.
LOOPBefore the first take: a length to stop at (FREE: when you press REC again). After: trims or extends the loop, a twentieth of a second a click.
LEVELThe tape's level into the mix.

LOOP (HOME again)

Trims the loop at both ends, inside the take you recorded: START (a hundredth of a second a click), END (a twentieth), SLIDE (moves the loop along the take, keeping its length) and FULL (turn it: the whole take again). The tracks show the whole take, with what's outside the loop dimmed and START and END marked. Nothing outside the loop is lost: open it up again whenever you like.

TRACK (HOME a third time)

The selected track (hold HOME and press its key, or turn ALGORITHM): its own SPEED (half to double, on top of the tape's), PITCH (±12 semitones, on top of the tape's), DUB level and LEVEL. Tracks at different speeds drift against each other round the loop; PLAY from stopped lines them up again. In the first take and in a bounce's print the tracks run at the tape's speed. At HI the tape and a track together reach double speed at most.

TAPE SPEED (PRESETS)

SpeedRatePer track
HI22.05 kHz2.39 s
MID16 kHz3.3 s
LO11.025 kHz4.78 s

Lower is longer and darker, as on a cassette. Switching with something on the tape plays it at the new speed: an octave up from LO to HI.

07

Recording

  • Arm a track with its key (F3, G3, A3, B3). Its lamp lights, and blinks while it records. One track is armed at a time: arming another disarms the first (and punches it out if it was recording).
  • The first take: on an empty tape, REC starts recording into the armed tracks; REC or PLAY ends it and the loop plays round. If LOOP is set, the take ends there by itself; a full track ends it too.
  • Punching: while the loop plays, REC punches the armed tracks in, REC again punches them out. Stopped, REC starts the tape and punches in.
  • DUB or REPLACE (INPUT, PRESETS): DUB adds to what's on the track, REPLACE records over it (you hear only the input while punched in). A hot DUB is taken softly by the tape.
  • Monitoring (INPUT, K4): AUTO hears the input while a track is armed; ON always; OFF never.
  • Clearing: hold ARP (CLEAR) and press a track's key. Hold ARP and press PLAY to erase the whole tape (and the loop length).
  • DUB level (HOME, TRACK view): how much of what's on the track a DUB pass keeps. 100 %: all of it (sound on sound); lower lets older passes fade each time round; 0 %: each pass replaces.

Each pass is re-encoded, so a part that is overdubbed many times picks up a little hiss, as tape does.

08

Mixer (EDIT)

Four strips, each with its VU meter, what it holds (TAPE, ARMED, REC, EMPTY), TRIM with its preamp lamp, PAN and SEND, a fader and its ARM button. KNOB 1–4 work the four strips at once: EDIT steps through LEVEL, TRIM, PAN, then SEND (the row in use is lit), then on to INPUT.

SEND is each track's send to the delay and the verb, after its fader (a muted track sends nothing). All four start at zero. On the SEND view the faders flip to show the sends.

Mute and solo: on the mixer the track keys mute (F3, G3, A3, B3, lit while muted) and the next four white keys solo (C4, D4, E4, F4, lit while soloed). With any track soloed, the rest are silent; solo several to hear them together. Elsewhere the track keys arm.

TRIM drives the channel's preamp: 0 dB is clean; push it until the lamp glows for warmth, further for grit.

08b

Master (FX)

On everything you hear: the tape's mix and the monitored input.

EQ (the first four views)

Four bands, each FREQ (20 Hz to 20 kHz), GAIN (±15 dB), Q and TYPE. Bands 2 and 3 are bells; band 1 can be a bell, a low shelf or a LO CUT, band 4 a bell, a high shelf or a HI CUT (Q sets a cut's resonance). The screen draws the curve; a band at 0 dB is out of the way.

COMP (the last two views)

Ravel's glue compressor, after the SSL G bus compressor: COMP on or off, THRESH (−30 to 0 dB), RATIO (2:1, 4:1, 10:1), MAKEUP; then ATTACK (0.1 to 30 ms), RELEASE (0.1 to 1.2 s, or AUTO, two-stage), MIX (below 100 % the dry sound under it) and KEY, the sidechain: what it listens to. MIX, a track (after its strip, before its fader and mute, so a muted track can still push the rest down) or INPUT (the live input, monitored or not: talk or play over the tape and it ducks). The bar shows the gain reduction; the dot on the curve is the level going in.

DELAY and VERB (after the compressor's views)

Fed by the mixer's SENDs; their returns go into the master with the tape, before the EQ.

  • DELAY: lo-fi, 8 bits at a quarter rate, the repeats darkening and saturating. TIME (20 to 350 ms), FEEDBACK, MIX (its return) and TO VRB (how much of the repeats go on into the verb). The screen draws the repeats.
  • VERB: after the Alesis era's (Ravel's 12-BIT plate, one tank): four combs and two allpasses a side at half rate, held in 12 bits, so it lisps and aliases as early digital verbs did. SIZE (how long it rings), DAMP (how dark), MIX (its return) and BITS: 12, 10 or 8, grittier each step. The screen draws the tail in its steps.

Their memory came from the tape: a track was 2.8 s at HI before 2.6.

09b

Motion and slice (SEL)

MOTION

The TAPE screen again, with knobs that can be recorded: SPEED, PITCH, RESON's MIX and LEVEL; SEL again for the selected track's SPEED, PITCH, PAN and LEVEL. Press REC (the tape plays if it was stopped) and turn knobs: each one you turn is written round the loop, following your hand, until you press REC again. From then on it moves by itself every pass. Up to four knobs can have motion; one that has shows an M before its name and its value moving. Recording again over a knob replaces its motion from where you take hold of it. ARP + a knob erases that knob's motion; ARP + REC erases all of it. Motion is saved with the project.

SLICE

Cuts a track's loop into slices and puts them on the white keys (F3 up): SOURCE (the track), SLICES (4, 8 or 16), SNAP (GRID: even slices; HITS: each cut moved to the strongest hit near it) and TO (where a take goes: FREE, or a track). The screen draws the loop and the cuts. While the page is open the source's own tape is silent, and its slices play through its strip (its level, pan, trim, pitch and speed).

REC records what you play into the free track, round the loop, until REC again: a new loop made of the slices. The original stays on its track (the FM-1 has no room for a hidden copy), so ARP + REC clears the take to start over; when you're happy, clear the original (ARP + its key) to free its track.

10

Bounce (SEQ)

Prints the picked tracks to another track, through RESON and a preamp, one loop long: the 4-track way to free tracks and to print effects.

  1. Pick the sources with the tracks' keys (lit).
  2. Choose where it goes with PRESETS: FREE (the first empty track) or a track.
  3. Press REC. The tape goes to the start, plays one loop and prints it; you hear the print as it's made.
Knob
WETRESON's mix on the bounce.
DRIVEThe bounce's preamp.
LEVELThe print's level.
CLEARYES clears the sources after the print, so they're free for more.
TO, RESON (SELECT)Where it goes, and RESON in or out.

Only the picked tracks go into the print (nothing else, such as the monitored input). The sources' levels, speeds and pitches set what's printed. "NO FREE TRACK" means every track not being bounced holds something: clear one (ARP + its key).

10b

Splice (SEQ, after BOUNCE)

Tracks 3 and 4 become two spools, and you print one onto the other through a degrader, a section at a time, back and forth: generation loss by hand. Press SEQ past BOUNCE's two views. Record something on track 3 or 4 first (as any take).

  • Hold F#3: spool 2 prints onto spool 1, from where the tape is, for as long as you hold it. Hold G#3: spool 1 onto spool 2. A print is an overdub: the other spool's DUB knob (on HOME's TRACK view) sets how much of what was there stays. Hold it for a moment to splice a fragment in; for a whole loop to print it all.
  • A#3 and C#4 mute spool 1 and spool 2 (lit when muted).
  • The degrader is on the spool you print FROM (its reel lit), so you hear what will print. PRESETS swaps FROM without printing.
  • A new generation: printing the other way round (or swapping FROM) starts one: the eight knobs go back to zero and GEN counts up. Splices the same way keep the knobs.
View (SELECT)Knobs
WEARWOW (the spool's speed wandering, and flutter), SAT (saturation), AGE (the top end going, hiss), DROP (dropouts: gaps, more and deeper).
BREAKCRUSH (fewer bits, a lower rate), LOSSY (three bands, each moment the quiet ones dropped and the top flickering, as a starved codec), CMP (a comparator: past its threshold a hard square at the level), XOR (the top bits of what's on the other spool, under the print, flip the bits of this one: the spool's own past, scrambled in).
SCRUBWhile a print is held: a hand on the source, as on a record. FINGER pushes it (a flick speeds it up or slows it, settling back); pull back far enough and it plays reversed, push forward far enough and it plays forward again. DRAG holds it back toward a stop. Let go of the key and the hand comes off. REEL 1 and REEL 2 (KNOB 3, 4): the spools' levels.

The spools are tracks 3 and 4: they play in the mixer, and the robots and LFO matrix can move the degrader's knobs.

11

RESON (ENV)

Eight resonant bands, from 200 Hz to 4.8 kHz, that filter the sound, vocode it, ring from its transients and throw particles. ALGORITHM puts it where you want it: OFF, on a track's playback (TRK 1–4), or on the INPUT (so it goes to tape). A bounce with RESON in takes it for the print.

Knob
MODEA profile: LIQUID (watery, moving), GLASS (bright rings), THROAT (vowel-like vocoding), DUST (pings and specks: the default), RADIO (a narrow band), DROPS (droplets of its own sound). Turning it sets the bands, mix, decay and width to suit.
MIXDry to wet.
DECAYHow long the bands ring.
WIDTHHow narrow the bands are.
SHIFT, DUST, BAND (SELECT)The whole bank up or down an octave; how many specks; the picked band's level (the white keys C4 to C5 pick it, PRESETS too).

The keys outside the tracks' and bands' ping it. The lamps over the sliders light as the bands ring.

ENV, third view

TAPE

Ravel's worn reel-to-reel, brought over from Ravel as it is: the sound read back from a short loop of tape by two heads, the tape's speed swaying as a tired motor's does, with a little flutter, the second head swaying partly the other way so the sound doubles into a slow, detuned chorus; and the tape's age: the highs worn away, the tape driven, its hiss under the note. At its defaults it matches, by measurement, a slowed tape-synth lead. Press ENV past RESON's two views.

  • WHERE: OFF, a track (TRK 1–4, after its preamp, as it plays), the INPUT (what you hear and record), or the BOUNCE (printed through it). There's one TAPE: put it where you want it.
  • TUNE: the wow's rate, 0.3 to 7 Hz (1.5 at the middle). AMOUNT: how far it sways (up to 3.3 ms), and with it the age: darker, driven, more hiss. MIX: dry to all tape.
  • The reference settings are the defaults: TUNE 64, AMOUNT 96, MIX 127. The screen draws the sway as it goes, and the wear (what's left of the highs).

Its memory came from the tape: a track is 2.39 s at HI now.

12

Robots (LFO)

Two robots move things on their own. Each has a target (PRESETS): SPEED, PITCH, the tape's level, the input's drive, a track's level or pan, or RESON's mix, decay, width or shift. Robot 1 starts on SPEED and robot 2 on PITCH, at no depth.

Knob
RATE0.02 to 10 Hz.
SHAPESINE, TRI, SAW, SQUARE, S+H, DRIFT, or GESTUR (a recorded gesture).
SKEWLeans the shape (or the square's width).
DEPTHHow far it moves. 0: off.

Gestures

Hold HOME and turn a knob whose parameter a robot can move (SPEED, PITCH, a level...). Let go of HOME: the movement, up to about 4 s, loops on a robot, and the knob goes back to where it was. ALGORITHM or SELECT shows the other robot.

Routing a robot to any knob

Besides its own target, either robot can move up to eight more knobs, shared between the two.

  • Hold LFO and turn a knob, on any page: robot 1 (or the one last shown on ROBOTS) moves that knob, by as much as you turn it: right for one way, left for the other. Turn it back to zero to remove it. The knob itself stays where it was. Still holding LFO, PRESETS picks robot 1 or 2. A tap of LFO still opens ROBOTS.
  • LFO MATRIX (ROBOTS, SELECT past the two robots): the eight routes, each with its robot, the knob, the depth and the robot's swing now. PRESETS picks a row; the knobs are FROM (LFO 1, LFO 2, or off), TO, DEPTH and DELETE. Turning any knob of an empty row starts one.

A route can move the tape's SPEED, PITCH and LEVEL, the input's TRIM and DRIVE, each track's TRIM, PAN, LEVEL, SPEED, PITCH and DUB, the bounce's DRIVE and LEVEL, and RESON's knobs and band levels. Not the master EQ and compressor, whose curves are worked out only when a knob moves, nor switches. Routes are saved with the project; projects saved by 2.4 load with an empty matrix.

13

MIDI

MIDI arrives over the MIDI jack, USB and Bluetooth MIDI alike.

  • MIDI learn: hold SAVE and turn a knob, then move a controller. Or hold SAVE and press REC, PLAY or a track's key, then play a note: that note does it from then on. The mappings belong to the FM-1, and a learned CC picks up: the parameter doesn't move until the controller passes its value. They're listed on ROBOTS (SELECT to the end); K4 clears the picked one.
  • Notes ping RESON and set its oscillator.
  • Start and stop run the tape.
  • KNOB CC: CC 110–113 turn knobs 1–4 of the page on screen (SETTINGS can switch it off).

14

Projects (SAVE)

Eight slots for the desk: every knob, the robots and their gestures. Not the tape, which stays in memory until the FM-1 switches off. PRESETS picks a slot; REC loads it, or saves into it if it's empty; SAVE + PLAY saves over it. Saving writes flash, so it only happens while stopped.

LOOK (GLO › SETTINGS, second view, KNOB 4)

IVORY (the default): a light cream-grey panel, dark windows set into it, mint on black, black knobs, white reels. SMOKE: the first look, a smoked body with cream lettering, aluminium knobs and amber.

KNOB SPEED (GLO › SETTINGS, second view, KNOB 3)

How far the knobs travel. Turn slowly for fine steps, quickly to cover ground. SLOW: a step a click; MEDIUM (the default): two a click on the 0–127 knobs, up to eight on a quick turn; FAST: three, up to twelve. Finer ranges (semitones and the like) keep single steps on a slow turn.

START WITH (GLO › SETTINGS)

LAST (the default) opens the project you last saved or loaded when the FM-1 switches on, and says what opened ("OPENED 3 ..."). It is the project as you last saved it, not what was playing when you switched off: there is no autosave. BLANK starts with the defaults. If the last start never ran properly (a crash or a hang), the next one starts blank and says "AFTER A RESET: NEW PROJECT", so a project can never stop the FM-1 starting; expect that once after an update too. A damaged slot starts blank and says so.

16

Trouble

Clicks and glitches

Clicks, even at zero volume, mean the FM-1 is out of CPU. The 4-Track is light (four tracks playing with an overdub and PITCH take about a sixth of the time in the emulator; RESON adds about a tenth), but if it happens a guard eases RESON in two steps, then rests the sound for about a second. GLO → SETTINGS shows the load. Turn RESON off (ALGORITHM on its page) to free the most.

Safe mode

"SAFE MODE" on the screen means the last three starts never ran for four seconds. Press PLAY to start Viscera anyway. Leaving it on for four seconds also clears the count, so the next power-on starts normally. Installing a new version starts the count afresh. In safe mode the sound is off and the install page still connects, so you can always reinstall. Hold OCT− + OCT+ while switching on to enter safe mode yourself.

A dark FM-1 after an install

If the FM-1 stays dark (no screen, no lights), Ravel's install page → Back to the stock firmware with a V15 file puts M-VAVE's firmware back.

Nothing records

Check INPUT: a green lamp on the source, the VU moving, and a track armed (the header says TO TRK n). REC on an empty tape with nothing armed says so.

Trying it without an FM-1

The browser emulator runs the same code, with an audio file, your computer's sound or a microphone as the input.
